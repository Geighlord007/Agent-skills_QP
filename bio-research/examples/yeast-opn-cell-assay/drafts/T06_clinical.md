# T6_clinical: Clinical and Preclinical Evidence for OPN Functions in Infants and Adults

**Draft date:** 2026-06-26  
**Scope:** Collect and organize clinical/preclinical evidence for human osteopontin (OPN; SPP1) functions, endpoints, effect sizes, study designs, and limitations. Focus on infants/pediatrics and adults. Exclude final recommendations. All factual claims are annotated with source IDs `[N]`.

---

## 1. Scope and Definitions

- This draft covers **orally ingested milk OPN** (mostly bovine milk OPN, bmOPN) in early life and **endogenous/plasma OPN** as a biomarker or therapeutic target in adults.
- It also records the limited **therapeutic-intervention** evidence in adults where OPN was directly blocked or administered.
- Confidence labels are applied where the evidence allows: **High / Medium / Low**.
- Gaps and contradictions are flagged explicitly.

---

## 2. Infant and Pediatric Evidence

### 2.1 Human Milk OPN Exposure and Natural Variability

- Human milk OPN (hmOPN) is among the most abundant breast-milk proteins. A multicenter study of 829 samples from 629 mothers (China, Denmark, Japan, Korea) reported medians of 99.7–266.2 mg/L depending on site and lactation stage; concentrations decline with infant age [17].
- Schack et al. (2009) reported ~138 mg/L hmOPN in mature milk, versus ~18 mg/L in bovine milk and ~9 mg/L in standard infant formula [19].
- Aksan et al. (2021, N=85 mother-infant pairs) found mean hmOPN 137.1 ± 56.8 mg/L and reported a moderate negative correlation between breast-milk OPN and fever-related infant hospitalizations at 0–3 months (r = −0.599, p < 0.001) [9]. **Confidence: Medium** (observational, single-center).
- Higher hmOPN was associated with vaginal delivery, non-obese postpartum BMI, non-smoking, and lower maternal energy/fat/carbohydrate/fiber intake [9][3].
- Plasma OPN is highest in neonates/infants and declines through childhood; adult levels are the lowest across the lifespan [10][3].

### 2.2 Randomized Controlled Trials in Healthy Term Infants

#### 2.2.1 Lönnerdal et al., 2016 (J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr; NCT00970398) [1]

- **Design:** Double-blind, randomized, parallel-arm; 279 infants in Shanghai; breastfed (BF) reference group plus formula-fed groups receiving standard formula (F0, ~14 mg/L bmOPN), F65 (~72 mg/L), or F130 (~150 mg/L) from 1–6 months.
- **Primary/clinical endpoints:** Growth, formula intake, stool consistency, adverse events, morbidity.
- **Biomarker endpoints:** Hematology, iron status, plasma amino acids, blood urea nitrogen, plasma cytokines, tetanus IgG at 6 months after DPT vaccination at 4 months.
- **Key findings:**
  - No significant differences in growth, formula intake, or adverse events among formula-fed groups [1].
  - F130 had lower plasma threonine and branched-chain amino acids, closer to BF profile [1].
  - Plasma TNF-α was higher in formula-fed than BF infants; among formula-fed groups, TNF-α was significantly lower in F65 and F130 vs F0 [1].
  - Incidence/prevalence of pyrexia (fever) was significantly lower in F130 vs F0 and comparable to BF; F65 intermediate [1][3].
  - Tetanus IgG response at 6 months was equivalent between F130, F0, and BF; F65 had fewer antibodies than F0 [3].
- **Limitations:** Single-center-ish (Shanghai); some infants in F65/F130 began formula before 1 month; short follow-up (6 months); modest effect sizes on clinical endpoints; concomitant medication endpoint not fully studied [1][3][4].

#### 2.2.2 West et al., 2017 (Pediatr Res) [2]

- **Design:** Secondary analysis of the Lönnerdal 2016 cohort; four-color flow cytometry of peripheral blood immune cells at 1, 4, and 6 months.
- **Endpoints:** Frequencies/composition of T cells, B cells, NK cells, monocytes, activation/memory markers.
- **Key findings:**
  - F130 had increased T-cell proportions vs F0 (P = 0.036, average effect size 0.51) and vs F65 (P = 0.008, average effect size 0.65) [2].
  - Monocyte proportions were increased in F65 and F130 vs BF but not vs each other [2].
  - No differences in white blood cells, lymphocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, T-helper/cytotoxic ratio, naïve/memory T cells, B cells, or NK cells [3].
- **Limitations:** Secondary analysis; effect sizes modest; clinical relevance of T-cell shift uncertain [3].

#### 2.2.3 Fleming et al., 2025 (PLOS ONE) [18]

- **Design:** Randomized, controlled, parallel-arm, double-blind, multicenter, non-inferiority trial (NCT03331276); Part 1 n=129, Part 2 n=117 receiving experimental formulas with OPN (among other bioactives), vs n=143 commercial formula for 120 days.
- **Primary endpoint:** Weight gain; non-inferiority margin > −3 g/d.
- **Key findings:**
  - Both experimental formulas were non-inferior for anthropometric outcomes [18].
  - Formula intake, adverse events, stool frequency/consistency similar to control; some measures of gassiness/fussiness improved (P < 0.05) [18].
- **Limitations:** OPN was co-administered with sn2 palmitate, GOS, FOS, polydextrose, β-carotene, lutein, α-lactalbumin, and lactoferrin; no OPN-specific attribution possible [18].

### 2.3 Preclinical Evidence in Early-Life Models

#### 2.3.1 OPN Knock-Out (KO) Mouse Cross-Fostering Studies [3]

- **Models:** WT or OPN-KO pups nursed by WT or OPN-KO dams; dietary OPN exposure ~12 mg/kg BW/day until PND 21.
- **Gut outcomes:** Pups without milk OPN had lower jejunal cell proliferation, smaller jejunal inner surface on PND 10/20, shorter intestine per body weight on PND 4–6, lower brush-border alkaline phosphatase, and fewer goblet, enteroendocrine, and Paneth cells post-weaning [3].
- **Brain outcomes:** Pups without milk OPN had lower brain OPN protein on PND 6/8, reduced oligodendrocyte differentiation, thinner myelin sheaths, and impaired rotarod/passive avoidance performance persisting after weaning [3].
- **Immune outcomes:** Pups without milk OPN showed higher plasma TNF-α after LPS challenge on PND 30 [3].
- **Rescue:** Oral bmOPN (12 mg/kg/day), recombinant bovine OPN, or recombinant human OPN reversed many of these phenotypes, suggesting functional similarity across species/forms [3]. **Confidence: Medium** (mouse model, high internal validity; translational uncertainty).

#### 2.3.2 Rat Study (Chen et al., cited in [3])

- Rats gavaged with standard formula (10 mg/L bmOPN) vs OPN-enriched formula (65 mg/L) from PND 7–28.
- bmOPN-fed rats had greater CD3+ cells in lymph nodes and anti-ovalbumin IgG shifted toward dam-fed profile; no difference in LPS-induced Ig response [3].

#### 2.3.3 Preterm Pig Models [6][7][3]

| Study | Model | Dose | Key Findings | Limitations |
|-------|-------|------|--------------|-------------|
| Aasmul-Olsen et al., 2021 [6] | Cesarean preterm pigs, raw bovine milk ± OPN, PND 1–19 | 46 mg/kg/day (median 319 mg/L) | Higher villus-to-crypt ratio; higher monocytes/lymphocytes on PND 8; lower neutrophil phagocytic rate on PND 19; no cognitive/brain differences | Minor effects; no consistent immune phenotype; antibiotics confounders |
| Møller et al., 2011 [7] | Preterm pigs with/without intra-amniotic LPS, OPN-enriched formula | 80 mg/kg/day initially, then 266.4 mg/kg/day × 1.5 d | Reduced severity of inflammatory intestinal injury; greater mannitol absorption | Short exposure; heterogeneous model |
| Siggers et al. (intra-amniotic LPS; cited in [3]) | Preterm pigs, OPN-enriched formula × 5 d | 53.3 mg/kg/day | No differences in intestinal injury, diarrhea, villus/crypt, enzymes, microbiota, blood chemistry, immune cell counts, or motor development | Negative trial for most endpoints |

- **Panel conclusion:** Preterm pig data are heterogeneous; effects on gut and immunity are modest at best [3].

#### 2.3.4 Rhesus Monkey Transcriptome Study (Donovan et al., 2014) [20]

- **Design:** Newborn rhesus monkeys breastfed (n=4), formula-fed (n=6), or formula + 125 mg/L bovine OPN (n=6) for 3 months.
- **Endpoints:** Growth, body composition, jejunal transcriptome.
- **Key findings:**
  - 1,017 genes differentially expressed between BF and FF; only 217 between BF and OPN-supplemented; bOPN shifted expression toward BF pattern [20].
  - Enriched pathways: cell adhesion, cytoskeletal remodeling, Wnt signaling, neuronal development [20].
  - No differences in hematology parameters [20].
- **Limitations:** Small sample; transcriptome endpoint only; no clinical immune or cognitive outcomes [20].

## 3. Adult Evidence

### 3.1 OPN as a Biomarker

#### 3.1.1 Reference Ranges [10]

- Nourkami-Tutdibi et al. (2020, N=117 children + 19 adults) established pediatric/adult reference intervals.
- Highest OPN in cord blood/neonates; significant age-dependent decline (Spearman r = −0.874, p < 0.001) [10].
- Adult mean plasma OPN ~330 ng/mL; healthy adult ranges vary widely across assays (reported 31–>200 ng/mL) [10][11].
- **Confidence: Medium** for age trend; **Low** for absolute cutoffs due to assay heterogeneity.

#### 3.1.2 Multiple Sclerosis [15]

- CSF and plasma OPN are elevated in MS vs healthy controls; higher during relapses and in secondary-progressive MS [15].
- Meta-analysis (González-Jiménez et al., 2025, 29 studies): CSF OPN higher in MS vs HC; plasma OPN decreased significantly after ≥6 months of natalizumab (p = 0.002), normalizing toward HC levels [15].
- OPN lacks specificity for MS (elevated in other neurological/inflammatory conditions) and is not recommended as a standalone diagnostic marker [15].

#### 3.1.3 Cancer

- Plasma OPN is elevated in hepatocellular carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, colorectal cancer, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, and metastatic breast cancer; higher levels often correlate with tumor burden and metastasis [11][12].
- DAHANCA 5 trial (Overgaard et al., 2005): plasma OPN tertiles predicted benefit from hypoxia radiosensitizer nimorazole in head-and-neck cancer. High OPN patients had large benefit (locoregional failure RR 0.19, 95% CI 0.08–0.44); low/intermediate OPN patients did not [13].
- **Confidence: Medium** for prognostic association; **Low** for diagnostic specificity.

#### 3.1.4 Cardiovascular and Other Conditions

- Elevated OPN associated with coronary artery disease severity, but overlap with other inflammatory conditions limits diagnostic utility [12].
- Plasma OPN positively correlates with inflammatory cytokines and negatively with neurocognitive performance in vascular cognitive impairment and Alzheimer’s disease [12].
- Pediatric traumatic brain injury: admission plasma OPN predicted mortality (cutoff ≥218.63, OR 9.91, 95% CI 3.35–29.36; sensitivity 0.69, specificity 0.82) [11].

### 3.2 Therapeutic Trials Targeting OPN

#### 3.2.1 ASK8007 Anti-OPN Monoclonal Antibody in Rheumatoid Arthritis [12]

- **Design:** Double-blind, multicentre, combined phase I (single-dose escalation) and phase IIa (multiple-dose proof-of-concept); RA patients with active disease.
- **Endpoints:** Safety/tolerability; PK/PD; DAS28 change; CD68+ synovial sublining macrophages.
- **Key findings:**
  - ASK8007 was safe and well tolerated up to 20 mg/kg; quantifiable in synovial fluid [12].
  - **No differences** in DAS28 or CD68 macrophages vs placebo [12].
  - No apparent clinical responses or biomarker changes within the ASK8007 group [12].
- **Conclusion:** OPN blockade is unlikely to induce robust clinical improvement in RA [12].
- **Confidence: High** for RA (adequately powered phase IIa negative trial).

#### 3.2.2 Other Adult Supplementation Trials

- **Bovine OPN for elderly immune support (NIZO/Arla, ISRCTN39995710; ClinicalTrials.gov-linked registration):** double-blind RCT in healthy elderly, 2.5–3.2 g/day Lacprodan OPN-10 vs maltodextrin for 14 weeks; primary endpoint = hepatitis B vaccine responder rate. Recruitment status reported as stopped; no peer-reviewed results identified [11].
- **Selenium/CoQ10 trial:** OPN measured as an inflammation biomarker in elderly community-dwellers; supplementation significantly lowered OPN along with other inflammatory markers at 6 and 42 months [11]. Not an OPN intervention.

## 4. Regulatory and Patent Landscape

### 4.1 European Union

- **EFSA NDA Panel opinion (2022):** Bovine milk OPN (Lacprodan OPN-10) is safe as a novel food at up to 151 mg/L in infant formula, follow-on formula, and ready-to-eat dairy-based meals for children up to 35 months [4].
  - Noted inconsistencies/limitations in the 6-month infant study but concluded no safety concerns [4].
  - Margin of exposure: NOAEL 1,200 mg/kg/day from rat subchronic toxicity vs P95 infant intake 33.4 mg/kg/day (MOE ≈ 36) [4].
- **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/463:** Authorized bovine milk osteopontin in the EU novel foods list at 151 mg/L; mandatory label name “Bovine milk Osteopontin” [16].
- **EU 2017/2470 updated union list** also includes the 151 mg/L maximum [16].

### 4.2 United States

- **FDA GRAS Notice GRN 716 (Arla Foods Ingredients, 2017):** Proposed use in cow’s milk-based infant formula and powdered beverages for children 1–3 years at a 90th-percentile intake of 39.5 mg/kg/day [5].
- **Status:** FDA “ceased to evaluate” the notice at the notifier’s request in February 2018 [5].
- **FDA concerns (per FOIA memos and Fleming 2024):** high variability of hmOPN exposure; relevance of selected bmOPN level; lack of ADME data; potential BBB crossing; long-term immunomodulatory role/mechanism; functional similarity to hmOPN; inadequacy of standard toxicology; potential relationship to immune-related diseases [3][5].

### 4.3 Australia/New Zealand (FSANZ)

- No FSANZ approval specifically for bovine milk osteopontin as a novel food or nutritive substance in infant formula was identified in this search. FSANZ has approved related bovine whey proteins (e.g., lactoferrin, A1253, 2023) [11], but OPN was not found.
- **Gap:** FSANZ regulatory status for bmOPN remains unconfirmed.

### 4.4 Patents

- **US Patent 11,897,950 (issued 13 Feb 2024):** Anti-OPN monoclonal antibodies and antigen-binding fragments for modulating OPN signaling; proposed indications include cancer, osteoporosis, and immune diseases; describes combination therapy with checkpoint inhibitors [14].
- **Arla Foods Ingredients patents:** Holds patents for production and application of milk OPN in infant nutrition (noted in trade press) [11].

## 5. Key Contradictions and Gaps

### 5.1 Contradictions / Inconsistencies

1. **Infant clinical efficacy:** Lönnerdal 2016 reported lower pyrexia with F130 [1], but the expert panel noted “no or minimal differences” between F0 and F130 for clinically relevant immune endpoints overall [3]. The difference is partly semantic: fever incidence was statistically significant, but the panel judged it modest in clinical context.
2. **Preterm pig effects:** Møller 2011 reported reduced intestinal injury with OPN [7], whereas Siggers (intra-amniotic LPS model) found no difference in intestinal injury or diarrhea [3]. Models, doses, and durations differed.
3. **BBB transport:** Mouse KO studies detected OPN protein in whole brain after oral exposure [3], but whether intact OPN crosses the BBB is disputed; radiolabel studies cannot distinguish intact protein from peptides/amino acids [3].
4. **Adult therapeutic targeting:** OPN is widely described as pro-inflammatory and pro-tumorigenic [11][12], yet blocking OPN with ASK8007 in RA showed **no clinical benefit** [12], suggesting redundancy or context-specific roles.

### 5.2 Evidence Gaps

1. **Long-term infant follow-up:** No RCTs track OPN-supplemented infants beyond 6 months for growth, immune, cognitive, or metabolic outcomes [3][4].
2. **ADME in infants:** Limited data on absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion; dietary bmOPN represents <5% of circulating OPN, and mechanism of action remains unclear [3][8].
3. **Dose–response:** Optimal OPN concentration in formula is uncertain; 65 mg/L produced weaker signals than 130 mg/L for some endpoints [1][2].
4. **Functional equivalence:** Recombinant human/bovine OPN and bmOPN show similar effects in mouse models [3], but glycosylation/phosphorylation differences may matter for human infants; direct comparative clinical data absent.
5. **Adult therapeutic utility:** No positive phase II/III trials of OPN agonists or antagonists with robust clinical endpoints; ASK8007 was negative [12].
6. **FSANZ status:** Unconfirmed whether bmOPN is approved or under assessment in Australia/New Zealand.
7. **Assay standardization:** Plasma OPN reference ranges vary widely across ELISAs, limiting cross-study comparability [10][11].
