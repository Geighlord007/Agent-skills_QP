# T7 Regulatory: Precedents for OPN and Recombinant Milk Proteins

**Project:** Yeast-Expressed Human Osteopontin (OPN) — Recommended Cell Assays  
**Draft purpose:** Collect and organize regulatory precedent data. *Not a final report.*  
**Date compiled:** 2026-06-26

---

## 1. Scope and Search Strategy

- **Objective:** Identify regulatory precedents for osteopontin (OPN) and for recombinant/precision-fermentation-derived milk proteins, with emphasis on FDA GRAS notices (GRN 716, 1219, 1284), EFSA novel-food opinions, FSANZ approvals, and relevant patents.
- **Sources prioritized:** FDA GRAS inventory and response letters; EFSA scientific opinions; EU implementing regulations; FSANZ application materials; peer-reviewed papers; patent documents.
- **Confidence legend used throughout:**
  - **High:** Directly from an official agency document or peer-reviewed primary data.
  - **Medium:** Industry/trade reporting or secondary summary that references official documents.
  - **Low:** Single-source or preliminary information requiring verification.

## 2. FDA GRAS Precedents

### 2.1 GRN 716 — Bovine Milk Osteopontin (Arla Foods Ingredients)

| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|-----------|---------|------------|
| Substance | Bovine milk osteopontin (bmOPN) isolated from bovine whey | High [1] |
| Notifier | Arla Foods Ingredients Group P/S, Denmark | High [1] |
| Intended use | Non-exempt cows’ milk-based infant formula and cows’ milk powdered beverages for children 1–3 y; target 90th-percentile intake of 39.5 mg/kg bw/day | High [1] |
| Basis | Scientific procedures | High [1] |
| Date of closure | 1 Feb 2018 | High [1] |
| FDA outcome | **“At the notifier’s request, FDA ceased to evaluate this notice.”** (not a “no questions” letter) | High [1] |
| Key FDA concerns (from subsequently released internal memos) | (1) High variability of human-milk OPN exposure; (2) relevance of selected bmOPN level; (3) lack of ADME data; (4) potential blood–brain-barrier crossing; (5) long-term immunomodulatory role/MOA; (6) functional similarity to human milk OPN; (7) inadequacy of standard toxicological assessments for bioactive ingredients; (8) potential relationship to immune-related diseases | High [2][3] |

**Interpretation for yeast-expressed human OPN:** GRN 716 is the closest regulatory precedent for adding OPN to infant formula, but it was *withdrawn* in the U.S. because FDA could not resolve concerns specific to bioactive ingredients in infant formula. The same evidentiary hurdles (ADME, immunomodulation, developmental neuro/immune endpoints) are likely to apply to any recombinant OPN intended for infant nutrition.

### 3.3 EU QPS Status of Common Production Hosts

- *Komagataella phaffii* (formerly *Pichia pastoris*) is widely used for food enzymes and holds **Qualified Presumption of Safety (QPS)** status in the EU for production of food enzymes/processing aids, subject to absence of the production organism and its DNA in the final product [13].
- *Trichoderma reesei* is also a well-established host for food enzymes under EU assessments [10][13].

**Observation:** As of the documents reviewed, FSANZ has approved precision-fermented *minor* food components (enzymes, HMOs, heme) but has not yet approved a precision-fermented *major* dairy protein [14][16]. Eden Brew’s beta-casein filing is the test case.

### 4.3 FSANZ Process vs. Product Focus

- **FSANZ** regulates the *process* (genetically modified production organism) under Standard 1.5.2, and final products may be classified as genetically modified even if no production organism remains [16].
- **FDA** generally regulates the *product* (purified protein), so the GRAS notification focuses on identity, purity, and safety of the final protein [16].
- **EU** requires absence of the production organism and its DNA in the final product for precision-fermented novel foods [16].

## 5. Recombinant / Yeast-Expressed OPN Specifically

### 5.1 Recombinant OPN Produced in *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii*

- Jiang, Tran & Lönnerdal (2021) reported that **recombinant bovine OPN and recombinant human OPN** produced in the alga *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* exhibited bioactivities similar to bovine milk OPN in OPN-deficient mouse pups [17].
- The recombinant OPNs were **not glycosylated** and had fewer phosphorylation sites than milk-derived bmOPN, yet showed comparable absorption and effects on brain and immune outcomes in the mouse model [3][17].
- **Regulatory status:** No food-grade regulatory submission or approval for algal recombinant OPN was identified.

### 5.2 Yeast-Expressed OPN Research and Patents

| Item | Finding | Confidence |
|------|---------|------------|
| UKRI funded project | “Milk-free production of recombinant Osteopontin to enable supplementation in baby, elderly, and sports nutrition” — aims to produce OPN from yeast *with post-translational modifications* using precision fermentation | Medium [18] |
| Chinese patent application CN114606148A | “Pichia pastoris strain for expressing osteopontin”; describes methanol-inducible expression of OPN in *Pichia pastoris* | Medium [19] |
| U.S. patent application 2020/0337336 | Recombinant *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and other microbes for animal feed, listing recombinant osteopontin among milk/animal proteins; **not food-use** | Medium [20] |

### 5.3 Regulatory Gap for Yeast-Expressed Human OPN

- **No FDA GRAS notice, EFSA novel-food opinion, or FSANZ approval for yeast-expressed human OPN was found.**
- The closest direct precedent is GRN 716 (bovine milk OPN), which was **not** cleared by FDA.
- The closest *host* precedents are GRN 1219/1284 (bovine lactoferrin in *K. phaffii*) and GRN 1200 (β-lactoglobulin in *K. phaffii*), but these are for bovine milk proteins and explicitly exclude infant formula.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Regulatory Themes

### 6.1 Common Requirements Across Jurisdictions

1. **Identity and sequence equivalence:** Amino-acid sequence identity to the native milk protein is a central argument (e.g., GRN 1219, GRN 1200) [4][8].
2. **Host safety:** Non-pathogenic, non-toxigenic production strain; absence of antibiotic-resistance markers; genetic stability confirmed by whole-genome sequencing or PCR [4][6][8].
3. **Purification / removal of host:** Centrifugation/filtration to remove biomass, chromatography, UF/DF; residual host-protein allergenicity assessed [4][6][8].
4. **Specifications:** Protein content, purity, moisture, ash, heavy metals, microbial limits; batch data from ≥3 non-consecutive lots [4][6][8].
5. **Allergen labeling:** Any protein identical or derived from a major allergen (milk) must be declared [4][6][8][10].
6. **Dietary exposure:** NHANES-based intake estimates at mean and 90th percentile for intended-use populations [4][6][8].
7. **Standards of identity / color-additive / 301(ll):** FDA response letters routinely flag that GRAS review does not address whether an ingredient may be used in standardized foods, whether it is a color additive, or whether Section 301(ll) of the FD&C Act applies [4][6][8][10].

### 6.2 Infant Formula as a Higher-Risk Category

- FDA explicitly distinguishes infant-formula uses from general food uses. GRN 1219 and GRN 1284 both state rbLf is **not intended for infant formula** [4][6].
- FDA’s 2020 internal memos on bioactive ingredients in infant formula (cited in Fleming et al., 2024) highlight concerns that standard toxicology may be insufficient for bioactive proteins and that clinical/developmental endpoints may be needed [2][3].
- EFSA, by contrast, cleared bmOPN for infant formula based on toxicology + one infant study, while noting limitations [11].

## 7. Gaps, Contradictions, and Risk Flags

### 7.1 Contradictions

| Issue | Jurisdiction A | Jurisdiction B |
|-------|----------------|----------------|
| OPN in infant formula | **EU/EFSA:** Safe at ≤151 mg/L for IF/FoF/young-children drinks [11][12] | **U.S. FDA:** GRN 716 withdrawn due to unresolved safety concerns specific to bioactive ingredients in infant formula [1][2][3] |
| Regulatory focus | **FDA:** product-focused (protein identity, purity, safety) | **FSANZ:** process-focused (GMO production organism) [16] |
| GMO labelling of final product | **U.S.:** generally not required for purified recombinant protein if no modified DNA remains | **Australia/NZ:** final food may be classified as GMO even if no production organism remains [16] |

### 7.2 Key Gaps for Yeast-Expressed Human OPN

1. **No food-regulatory precedent exists for recombinant human OPN from any host.**
2. **No yeast-expressed OPN food regulatory submission** was identified (only research-stage patents/projects).
3. **Post-translational modification (PTM) profile:** Human milk OPN is heavily phosphorylated and glycosylated; yeast glycosylation patterns differ from mammalian patterns. A yeast-expressed human OPN would need to demonstrate PTM equivalence (or justify differences) for safety and functional claims [3][11][17].
4. **ADME/bioactivity package:** The FDA’s GRN 716 concerns specifically cited lack of ADME data and uncertainty about blood–brain-barrier crossing and immunomodulation [2][3]. A yeast-expressed human OPN dossier would need to address these points, especially for infant use.
5. **Allergenicity of host residuals:** While K. *phaffii* has a long history of safe use and is considered non-allergenic, residual yeast proteins in the final isolate must be quantified and assessed (as done for GRN 1219/1284/1200) [4][6][8].
6. **Data-protection barrier in EU:** Arla holds five-year proprietary-data protection for bmOPN in the EU until March 2028 [12]. A subsequent yeast-expressed human OPN applicant would need to rely on publicly available data or negotiate rights, because the protected dossier includes key toxicology and infant-study reports [11][12].
7. **Terminology / common or usual name:** FDA response letters for recombinant milk proteins note that the agency’s use of a protein name (e.g., “β-lactoglobulin”) does not mean it is an appropriate common or usual name for labeling [8][10]. The same issue will apply to “osteopontin.”

### 7.3 Risk Flags

- **High regulatory risk for infant-formula use:** The U.S. pathway for OPN in infant formula is currently blocked by the GRN 716 precedent; expect FDA to require extensive developmental-safety data.
- **Moderate regulatory risk for general food use:** Precedents for K. *phaffii*-expressed bovine milk proteins (lactoferrin, β-lactoglobulin) in general foods are positive, but OPN is a bioactive phosphoglycoprotein with immunomodulatory properties, which may attract greater scrutiny than a nutritional protein.
- **Moderate risk in EU/FSANZ:** Novel-food approval is feasible (bmOPN precedent), but a yeast-expressed human OPN would likely be considered a *different* novel food from bmOPN, requiring its own full safety dossier.
