# T5: Safety and Toxicology Precedent for OPN and Recombinant Milk Proteins — Draft Data Collection

**Scope:** Collect safety/toxicology precedent relevant to a yeast-expressed human osteopontin (OPN) intended for use as a cell-culture/food ingredient. Focus areas are the genotoxicity package, allergenicity, 90-day subchronic studies, and host-cell-protein (HCP) / host-cell-DNA clearance. This is a structured data collection, **not** a final safety assessment.

**Search strategy:** WebSearch + FetchURL; prioritized PubMed/PMC, FDA/EFSA/FSANZ/Health Canada/WHO official documents, peer-reviewed papers, and patents. Press releases were avoided for key safety claims.

## 2. OPN safety precedent — bovine milk OPN (bmOPN)

### 2.1 Exposure context and regulatory status

- bmOPN is present at ~18 mg/L in cow’s milk and ~5–13 mg/L in conventional cow’s-milk-based infant formula; human milk OPN is reported at ~65–250 mg/L, with higher values in early lactation [1,3].
- EFSA approved bmOPN as a novel food at a maximum use level of **151 mg/L** in infant formula, follow-on formula, and ready-to-eat dairy-based meals for children up to 35 months. This corresponds to ~109 mg bmOPN/L formula [1].
- The highest estimated P95 infant intake was **33.4 mg/kg bw/day**, giving a margin of exposure (MOE) of ~36 vs. the rat NOAEL of 1,200 mg/kg bw/day [1]. EFSA considered this MOE sufficient given the source, the toxicological data, and the low circulating bmOPN levels in infants [1].
- **FDA GRN 716** for bmOPN in infant formula was **withdrawn by the notifier** after FDA raised concerns about: high variability of human-milk OPN exposure; relevance of the chosen use level; lack of ADME data; potential blood–brain-barrier crossing; long-term immunomodulatory effects; and inadequacy of standard toxicology endpoints for a bioactive ingredient in infants [2,21].
- An independent expert panel concluded that available data do not raise safety concerns, but identified gaps in ADME and recommended prioritizing clinical trials with comprehensive immune and cognitive endpoints [3].

**Confidence:** High for EFSA’s conclusions and FDA’s stated concerns; Medium for the infant clinical data because of inconsistencies/limitations noted by EFSA [1].

### 2.2 Genotoxicity package for bmOPN

| Study | Guideline / system | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bacterial reverse mutation assay | OECD TG 471; *S. Typhimurium* TA98, TA100, TA1535, TA1537 + *E. coli* WP2 uvrA; ±S9; up to 5,000 µg/plate | Non-mutagenic [1] |
| In vitro mammalian chromosome aberration test | OECD TG 473; human peripheral blood lymphocytes; ±S9; up to 5,000 µg/mL | Non-clastogenic; no cytotoxicity [1] |
| In vivo mammalian micronucleus test | OECD TG 474; NMRI mice; 2,000 mg/kg bw/day oral gavage | No structural/numerical chromosomal damage [1] |

- EFSA noted that an *in vitro* micronucleus assay (recommended in its 2011 genotoxicity strategy) was **not performed**, but considered the overall package adequate given the source and nature of the protein [1].
- A 2025 review independently summarized Lacprodan® OPN-10 as non-mutagenic, non-clastogenic, and non-genotoxic in the micronucleus test [4].

**Confidence:** High.

### 2.3 90-day subchronic toxicity

- Study: OECD TG 408, GLP, Wistar rats, 10/sex/group, dietary inclusion at 0, 300, 600, or **1,200 mg/kg bw/day** for 13 weeks [1].
- Findings: no deaths; no test-item-related clinical signs; no ophthalmological changes; no effects on body weight, food/water consumption, or functional observation battery. Clinical chemistry showed no statistically significant differences. A few isolated hematology/urinalysis/organ-weight changes were considered incidental because of small magnitude, lack of dose-response, or absence at the high dose [1].
- NOAEL: **1,200 mg/kg bw/day** (highest dose tested) [1].
- Limitation: the study was performed in 2007 and did not include TSH, T3, T4, or thyroid gland weight (later OECD TG 408 requirements). Thyroid histology was available and showed no effect; serum samples were no longer available for retrospective hormone analysis [1].

**Confidence:** High for the NOAEL; Medium for thyroid-endocrine completeness.

### 2.4 Reproductive/developmental toxicity

- A non-GLP teratogenicity study in Wistar rats (Chinese CDC, 2009) used gavage doses of 0, 630, 1,250, or 2,500 mg/kg bw/day on gestation days 7–16. No adverse effects on dams, fetal body weight/length, litter parameters, or visceral/skeletal examinations were reported [1].

**Confidence:** Medium (non-GLP, limited OECD compliance) [1].

### 2.5 Infant clinical data

- A 6-month RCT (n=279) compared formula with 14, 72, or 140 mg OPN/L vs. breastfed infants. EFSA noted that, despite inconsistencies and limitations, the results did not raise safety concerns: no deaths/serious adverse events, no differences in growth, formula intake, or stool consistency [1,3]. Some immune-marker differences (IL-2, IL-10, T-cell subsets) were observed but were not interpreted as adverse [1,3].

**Confidence:** Medium due to study limitations flagged by EFSA [1].

### 2.6 ADME and functional equivalence

- Orally consumed OPN is at least partially resistant to gastric digestion, can be absorbed, and appears in circulation as intact protein/peptides [1,3].
- In infants fed bmOPN, circulating bmOPN was ~2–5% of endogenous human OPN; no significant impact on endogenous hOPN plasma levels was demonstrated [1,3].
- Recombinant bovine and human OPN produced in *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* showed similar absorption and bioactivity profiles to bmOPN in mouse pups, supporting functional bioequivalence across production systems [3].

**Confidence:** Medium; blood–brain-barrier transport remains unresolved [3].

### 2.7 Allergenicity

- OPN is **not** one of the major milk allergens (caseins, β-lactoglobulin, α-lactalbumin, bovine serum albumin) [1,5].
- EFSA’s dossier did not contain OPN-specific allergenicity studies. Because the source is bovine whey, products must comply with milk-allergen labeling requirements [1].
- One in silico/in vitro digestion study identified osteopontin fragments in milk hydrolysates but no allergenic fragments longer than 9 amino acids [25].

**Confidence:** Low–Medium for OPN-specific allergy risk; High for the need for milk-allergen labeling.

## 3. Recombinant milk-protein precedents

### 3.1 β-Lactoglobulin (bovine whey-derived)

- Identity: ≥90% protein, ≥90% BLG, chemically/structurally equivalent to bovine milk BLG [5].
- Genotoxicity: Ames (OECD 471) and in vitro mammalian micronucleus (OECD 487) both negative [5].
- 14-day range-finding study in Wistar rats (0/100/300/1,000 mg/kg bw/day) showed some transient WBC and kidney findings that were not reproduced in the 90-day study [5].
- 90-day OECD TG 408 study in Wistar rats: NOAEL **1,000 mg/kg bw/day** (highest dose). Some statistically significant findings (bedding moving, bilirubin, TSH, RBC/HCT, kidney weights) were considered not adverse because values remained within historical ranges and/or lacked histopathological correlates [5].
- Allergenicity: BLG is a major milk allergen (Bos d 5); products must be labeled for milk [5].

**Confidence:** High.

### 3.2 Recombinant β-LG from *Komagataella phaffii* (Remilk)

- Production strain: *K. phaffii* yRMK-66, derived from CBS 7435 (NRRL Y-11430), with a codon-optimized bovine β-LG gene integrated at the AOX1 locus [7].
- Equivalence: amino-acid sequence identical to bovine β-LG; SDS-PAGE/HPLC/SEC retention times nearly identical; circular dichroism/fluorescence showed high structural similarity; >99.4% of total protein as r-βLG; no post-translational modifications detected [7].
- Toxicology: Ames OECD 471 non-mutagenic; in vitro micronucleus OECD 487 non-clastogenic; Health Canada accepted the EFSA milk-BLG 90-day NOAEL of 1,000 mg/kg bw/day by equivalence [7].
- HCP/DNA: Health Canada noted that removal of cellular biomass during purification minimizes HCP; residual yeast proteins detected by LC-MS/MS "presented no safety concerns" [7].
- Allergenicity: milk-allergic individuals will react; product must carry milk allergen label. Bioinformatics on residual *K. phaffii* proteins (AllergenOnline v21, Codex guidelines) found no relevant matches [7].

**Confidence:** High.

### 3.3 Recombinant β-LG from *Trichoderma reesei* (Perfect Day / GRN 863)

- FDA issued a "no questions" GRAS letter (GRN 863) for β-LG produced by submerged fermentation of a genetically modified *T. reesei* strain [6].
- Intended use: substitute for dairy/plant proteins in foods; **explicitly not for use in infant formula or USDA-regulated products** [6].
- The notifier concluded the product is identical to commercially available bovine β-LG and therefore carries the same milk-allergen labeling obligation [6].

**Confidence:** High for regulatory status; Medium for detailed study data because the full dossier is not publicly summarized beyond the FDA letter.

### 3.4 Recombinant human lactoferrin from *K. phaffii* (Effera™)

- rhLF is a glycoprotein with known immunomodulatory/iron-homeostasis activity. Earlier FDA reviews of Helaina’s rhLF were withdrawn because of insufficient safety data and unresolved questions about immunogenicity/immunotoxicity of orally ingested rhLF [9].
- A subsequent **28-day GLP rat study** (10 rats/sex/group + toxicokinetic cohort) administered 0/200/600/2,000 mg/kg bw/day rhLF or 2,000 mg/kg bw/day bovine LF. No toxicologically significant changes in clinical observations, body weight, ophthalmology, hematology, coagulation, clinical chemistry, urinalysis, immunotoxicity endpoints, organ weights, or histopathology were observed [10].
- Allergenicity package: LC-MS/MS showed ≥99% target protein and 36 residual *K. phaffii* proteins, each <0.05% abundance; bioinformatics (AllergenOnline v22, BLASTP) showed no significant allergen matches; pepsin digestion profile comparable to human milk LF [11].
- A scientific expert panel published a safety-study roadmap concluding that the proposed studies address the unanswered questions and that the framework is broadly applicable to other recombinant milk proteins with immunomodulatory functions [9].

**Confidence:** High for the 28-day/allergenicity data; Medium for final GRAS status because adult clinical data are still emerging.

### 3.5 Soy leghemoglobin from *Pichia pastoris* / *K. phaffii* (Impossible Foods)

- FDA GRN 737 and FSANZ A1186 approvals for a *P. pastoris*-derived soy leghemoglobin preparation (LegH Prep) used in plant-based meat analogues [8,23,24].
- Source organism safety: *P. pastoris* is a well-characterized, non-pathogenic, non-toxigenic yeast with a history of safe use for food enzymes [8].
- Allergenicity/toxicity: bioinformatics showed no significant homology to known allergens or toxins; proteins were susceptible to pepsin digestion and denatured at cooking/acidic conditions [8].
- Genotoxicity: Ames and in vitro micronucleus both negative [8,23,24].
- Toxicity: 28-day dietary rat study NOAEL 1,536 mg/kg bw/day (highest dose) [8]; 90-day dietary rat study NOAEL 4,798 mg/kg/day (males) and 5,762 mg/kg/day (females) [24].
- The preparation contains residual *Pichia* proteins and DNA; FSANZ accepted the safety case based on strain safety, low abundance, digestibility, and toxicology rather than a fixed HCP/DNA limit [8].

**Confidence:** High.

## 4. Genotoxicity package precedent — summary table

| Protein / source | Ames (OECD 471) | In vitro chromosomal aberration / micronucleus (OECD 473/487) | In vivo micronucleus (OECD 474) | Overall conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bmOPN (bovine whey) [1] | Negative up to 5,000 µg/plate | Negative (chromosome aberration) up to 5,000 µg/mL | Negative at 2,000 mg/kg bw/day | No genotoxic concern; in vitro micronucleus not performed |

**Implication for yeast-expressed hOPN:** A standard OECD genotoxicity battery (Ames + in vitro micronucleus; in vivo micronucleus optional depending on jurisdiction and protein nature) is the expected starting point. EFSA accepted bmOPN without the in vitro micronucleus test, but the deviation should be justified.

| BLG (bovine whey) [5] | Wistar rat, OECD TG 408 | 0, 100, 300, 1,000 mg/kg bw/day | 1,000 mg/kg bw/day | Some non-adverse organ-weight/clinical-chemistry changes |

## 6. Allergenicity precedent

### 6.1 Milk-protein allergenicity

- β-Lactoglobulin is classified as a major milk allergen (Bos d 5); α-lactalbumin, caseins, and BSA are also major [5,11].
- OPN is not considered a major milk allergen, but because it is a bovine whey protein, products containing bovine-derived OPN must carry milk allergen labeling [1,5].
- A yeast-expressed **human** OPN is not a bovine milk protein, but if intended to replace or mimic bovine/human milk OPN, milk-allergen labeling decisions will depend on final product composition and jurisdiction.

### 6.2 Codex weight-of-evidence approach

Regulatory reviews for novel recombinant proteins consistently apply Codex/FAO-WHO guidance:

1. **Source and history of exposure** — safe host strain, gene donor safety [8,11].
2. **Sequence homology** — compare the novel protein and residual host proteins to known allergens (e.g., AllergenOnline; ≥35% identity over 80 amino acids is a common trigger for further scrutiny) [8,11].
3. **Pepsin digestibility** — rapid digestion in simulated gastric fluid supports low allergenic risk [8,11].
4. **Serum IgE binding / clinical data** — usually reserved for cases where in silico/digestion data raise concern [11].

### 6.3 Residual host-protein allergenicity

| Product | Residual host proteins | Assessment outcome |
|---|---|---|
| r-βLG (*K. phaffii*) [7] | LC-MS/MS detected; “presented no safety concerns” | Health Canada accepted |
| rhLF (*K. phaffii*) [11] | ≥99% target; 36 residual *K. phaffii* proteins, each <0.05% | No significant allergen matches; low risk |
| LegH Prep (*P. pastoris*) [8] | Mixture of *Pichia* proteins in the preparation | Bioinformatics + pepsin digestion supported safety |

**Glycosylation consideration:** Yeast N-glycans are typically high-mannose oligomannose structures. Helaina mitigated potential immunogenicity by knocking out *OCH1* to limit mannose chain length [11]. Human OPN is heavily phosphorylated and O-glycosylated; yeast PTMs may differ and need comparability assessment.

## 7. Host-cell protein (HCP) and host-cell DNA clearance

### 7.1 Regulatory expectations

| Document | Key expectation |
|---|---|
| **ICH Q6B** [13] | HCP impurities should be minimized; a sensitive immunoassay detecting a broad range of HCPs is generally used. Host-cell DNA can be detected by direct analysis (e.g., qPCR/hybridization). Clearance studies (e.g., spiking) may replace routine testing if validated. |
| **WHO TRS 987 Annex 4** [12] | Purification must remove HCP, nucleic acids, viruses, and other impurities. Residual cellular DNA generally reducible to **<10 ng per dose**; once validated, routine DNA testing may be omitted. |
| **Ph.Eur. 2.6.34 / BP 2022** [14] | HCP assays should use broad-spectrum polyclonal antibodies; orthogonal methods (SDS-PAGE, Western blot, MS) recommended. Acceptance limits are case-by-case, often expressed as ng HCP per mg active substance (ppm). |
| **EMA / FDA positions** [15,26] | HCP must be monitored routinely or via validated clearance; residual DNA limits are product-specific but commonly referenced at **<10 ng/dose** for non-oncogenic continuous cells and **<100 pg/dose** for some parenteral/vaccine products. |
| **Singapore SFA novel-food guidance (2025)** [16] | For recombinant proteins: demonstrate sequence equivalence, assess allergenicity/toxicity bioinformatically, and confirm removal of GM cells and recombinant DNA. |

### 7.2 Industry precedent for residual HCP/DNA in recombinant food proteins

- **Remilk r-βLG:** >99.4% target protein by LC-MS/MS; residual yeast proteins detected but considered safe; production strain DNA removal expected as part of purification [7].
- **Helaina rhLF:** ≥99% target protein; 36 residual *K. phaffii* proteins, max single protein abundance ~0.045%; no allergenic concern; high purity achieved by microfiltration/diafiltration + cation exchange + spray drying [11].
- **Impossible LegH Prep:** the preparation intentionally contains *Pichia* host proteins and DNA; FSANZ accepted safety based on strain safety, low abundance, digestibility, and toxicology data rather than a fixed HCP/DNA specification [8].
- **Recombinant hepatitis B vaccine (yeast):** example of biopharmaceutical DNA clearance to **<10 pg/dose** via multi-step chromatography [17].

### 7.3 Practical considerations for yeast-expressed hOPN

- **HCP assay:** develop a product-specific or platform yeast-HCP ELISA (or use LC-MS/MS profiling) that detects the broad *S. cerevisiae*, *K. phaffii*, or *T. reesei* proteome as appropriate. Validate clearance through downstream unit operations.
- **Residual DNA:** target **<10 ng/dose** as a conservative starting point aligned with WHO/EU expectations [12,15]. For food-grade yeast proteins, some regulators may accept a case-by-case risk assessment rather than a strict pharmaceutical limit [8].
- **Endotoxin / cell-wall components:** depending on host and lysis step, monitor yeast endotoxin (LAL), β-glucans, and mannans.
- **PTM comparability:** because OPN function depends on phosphorylation and glycosylation, demonstrate that yeast-expressed hOPN is structurally/functionally equivalent to native hOPN; otherwise a full toxicology package may be required.

## 9. Data gaps and contradictions

1. **No GLP genotoxicity or 90-day package specifically for yeast-expressed human OPN.** The existing OPN package is for bovine whey-derived OPN and may not cover recombinant post-translational differences [1].
2. **FDA–EFSA divergence on bmOPN:** EFSA approved bmOPN for infant formula with MOE 36, whereas FDA considered standard toxicology insufficient for a bioactive protein in infants and questioned immunomodulation, ADME, and BBB crossing [1,2,21]. This signals that a yeast-expressed hOPN for infant use would likely face similar or greater scrutiny.
3. **OPN-specific allergenicity data are sparse.** There are no published serum-IgE binding or clinical oral-challenge studies for OPN; risk management currently relies on milk-allergen labeling [1].
4. **Host-cell protein/DNA limits are product-specific.** Unlike small-molecule genotoxic impurities, there is no universal ppm or ng/dose limit for food-grade recombinant proteins; regulators accept case-by-case risk assessments [8,12,13,14,15].
5. **PTM comparability.** Yeast may not replicate human OPN phosphorylation/O-glycosylation patterns. PTM differences could affect safety, allergenicity, and bioactivity and must be characterized before leveraging native OPN toxicology data [11].
6. **90-day study design update.** The bmOPN 90-day study lacked thyroid hormones; any new OPN 90-day study should follow the current OECD TG 408 [1,5].
7. **Long-term human data for recombinant milk proteins are limited.** rhLF has only a 28-day rat study and emerging adult clinical data; no 90-day study for rhLF was identified in this search [9,10].
