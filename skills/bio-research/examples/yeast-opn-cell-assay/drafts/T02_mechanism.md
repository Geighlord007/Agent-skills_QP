# T02_mechanism: Biological mechanisms of osteopontin (OPN) — data collection

**Scope:** Collect and organize mechanism-focused evidence for OPN activity in intestinal barrier function, immune modulation (moDC, macrophages, PBMC), bone mineralization, and integrin/CD44 receptor biology. This is a data-collection draft, not a final report.

**Search strategy:** PubMed/MEDLINE (via PubMed pages and NCBI E-utilities), FDA GRAS inventory/letters, EFSA novel-food opinion, and patent databases. Priority was given to peer-reviewed papers, FDA/EFSA official documents, and patents; press releases were not used as primary evidence.

---

## 1. Structural and receptor overview

### 1.1 Key structural motifs

- OPN (secreted phosphoprotein 1, SPP1) is a highly phosphorylated, acidic, sialylated glycoprotein and a prominent non-collagenous component of mineralized bone and dentin matrices [1].
- Functional motifs include: (i) a polyaspartic acid region and Ser/Thr phosphorylation sites that mediate binding to hydroxyapatite; (ii) a conserved RGD integrin-binding sequence; and (iii) a thrombin-cleavage site that exposes the cryptic SVVYGLR (human) / SLAYGLR (mouse) integrin-binding motif [1][17][26].
- Post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, glycosylation, transglutamination, proteolytic cleavage by thrombin/MMPs) strongly modulate OPN activity, receptor usage, and mineral-binding capacity [1][27].

### 1.2 Principal receptors

| Receptor | Binding site / requirement | Functional relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Integrin αvβ3 | RGD motif (plus divalent cation) | Osteoclast motility, podosome formation, bone resorption, cell adhesion/signaling [17][23] |
| Integrin α9β1 | SVVYGLR motif exposed after thrombin cleavage; also binds polymeric OPN | Neutrophil recruitment, cell adhesion [26][28] |
| Integrin α4β1 | Non-RGD region (reported for OPN splice variants) | Leukocyte adhesion/migration [27] |
| CD44 (especially variant isoforms) | C-terminal / non-RGD region; binding is enhanced by structural constraint/heparin | Cell migration, chemotaxis, immune-cell recruitment, tumor progression [27] |

- Integrin αvβ3 and CD44 can act cooperatively or independently depending on cell type and context; α9β1 may compete with CD44 for OPN binding [27].
- Confidence in the RGD/αvβ3 and SVVYGLR/α9β1 axes is **High** (multiple structural and functional studies). Confidence in the exact CD44-binding domain and the role of heparin/structural constraint is **Medium–High** [27].

## 2. Intestinal barrier

### 2.1 Tight-junction and epithelial protection

- In DSS-induced acute colitis, OPN-deficient mice showed higher disease activity, shorter colon length, worse histology, and lower colonic occludin expression than wild-type mice [3].
- siRNA-mediated OPN knockdown in Caco-2 monolayers reduced junctional occludin, redistributed occludin to intracellular compartments, lowered transepithelial electrical resistance (TER), and decreased the phosphorylated occludin fraction in the Triton X-100–insoluble (tight-junction) fraction [3].
- These data support a model in which OPN is required for occludin phosphorylation and proper tight-junction localization, thereby maintaining barrier integrity [3].

### 2.2 Synergistic/protective effects with lactoferrin

- In an LPS-induced mouse intestinal-barrier injury model, a lactoferrin (LF):OPN 1:5 combination reduced serum diamine oxidase (DAO) and D-lactate, decreased FITC-dextran translocation, increased villus length and crypt depth, improved tight-junction structure/expression, increased absorptive enterocyte numbers, and promoted crypt-cell proliferation/differentiation via Notch signaling [4].
- The combination was more effective than LF or OPN alone [4].

### 2.3 Milk OPN and intestinal development

- Oral milk OPN in mouse pups was relatively resistant to gastrointestinal digestion and promoted small-intestinal growth, brush-border alkaline phosphatase activity, and increased numbers of goblet, enteroendocrine, and Paneth cells [5].
- Mechanistically, milk OPN up-regulated jejunal integrin αvβ3 and CD44 expression and activated ERK, PI3K/Akt, Wnt, and FAK signaling [5].

### 2.4 Confidence and gaps

- The occludin-phosphorylation mechanism is supported by both in vivo and in vitro data; confidence **High** [3].
- Milk-OPN developmental effects are largely from one mouse lactation model; confidence **Medium–High** [5].
- Direct evidence that **yeast-expressed human OPN** preserves these intestinal mechanisms was not identified; this is a data gap.

## 3. Immune modulation

### 3.1 Monocyte-derived dendritic cells (moDC)

| Observation | Model / readout | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human moDCs synthesize OPN during differentiation from CD14+ monocytes; production tends to fall as DCs mature | Human monocyte-derived DC cultures | High | [6] |
| Anti-OPN antibody-treated DCs are smaller, express lower HLA-DR, CD86, and CD83, and undergo more apoptosis | Human moDC differentiation/maturation assay | High | [6] |
| OPN augments HLA-DR, co-stimulatory/adhesion molecules, IL-12, TNF-α, and allostimulatory capacity; primes naive Th cells toward Th1 | Human myeloid DCs / mixed lymphocyte reaction | High | [8] |
| In chronic hepatitis B, OPN deficiency in DCs impairs IL-12 production and CD80/CD86/HLA-DR expression upon HBV antigen stimulation | Human PBMC-derived DCs + HBV antigens; murine BMDCs | High | [9] |
| DC-derived OPN suppresses OVA-specific IgE and Th2 cytokine production in an IL-12–dependent manner | Murine OPN−/− DCs + CD4+ T cells | High | [10] |
| OPN suppresses CCR7-driven DC migration to draining lymph nodes and down-regulates T-cell priming | Murine immature DCs / JAWSII line + heat-killed Listeria | High | [11] |
| IL-10 up-regulates OPN in monocytes and moDCs, whereas IL-4/IL-13 inhibit OPN expression | Human monocytes and moDCs | Medium | [7] |

- **Interpretation:** OPN has both activating (Th1-promoting, maturation/survival) and regulatory (IL-12–dependent Th2 suppression, CCR7/migration dampening) effects on DCs. The net effect is context-dependent.

### 3.2 Macrophages

| Observation | Model / readout | Interpretation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| OPN deficiency reduced adipose-tissue macrophage infiltration in obese mice, but OPN−/− ATMs expressed more M1 and fewer M2 markers yet produced less TNF-α | High-fat diet–induced obesity in Spp1−/− mice; human MDMs | OPN is not a simple M1 inducer; it modulates phagocytic capacity | [12] |
| OPN-treated human MDMs showed elevated phagocytosis and ambiguous M1/M2 marker changes; LPS-induced inflammatory cytokine production was diminished | Human monocyte-derived macrophages | OPN may act as a phagocytic signal rather than a pro-inflammatory polarizer | [12] |
| Exogenous OPN after cerebral ischemia reduced iNOS+ M1 microglia and increased Arg1+ M2 microglia, lowering the M1/M2 ratio | Rat photothrombotic stroke | OPN can shift microglia/macrophages toward an M2-like phenotype in CNS injury | [13] |
| Macrophage-derived OPN promoted M2 polarization and type 2 inflammation during pulmonary cryptococcosis, impairing fungal clearance | Murine alveolar macrophages; CRISPR OPN KO macrophages | Macrophage-derived OPN can drive M2/type-2 responses in infection | [14] |
| Synovial fluid from knee OA patients drove M1 and M2 marker changes in PBMC-derived CD14+ cells | Human PBMC-derived macrophages | PBMC-derived macrophages are a tractable model, but OPN-specific mechanism not isolated | [15] |

- **Contradiction/ambiguity:** The direction of OPN’s effect on macrophage polarization is not uniform. In adipose tissue, OPN deficiency shifts cells toward a more pro-inflammatory surface profile but lower TNF-α secretion [12]; in CNS injury, OPN treatment shifts microglia toward M2 [13]; in fungal infection, macrophage-derived OPN promotes M2/type-2 responses [14]. Confidence in a universal M1 vs. M2 role is **Low**; effects appear model- and tissue-dependent.

### 3.3 PBMC / whole-blood mononuclear cells

- In cattle, OPN expression in non-stimulated PBMC was higher in subclinical MAP-infected cows than in controls, and OPN expression correlated with Th1/Th2 cytokine patterns in PBMC [16].
- Human PBMC-derived DCs (BDCA-1+) showed reduced CD80/CD86/HLA-DR and IL-12 when OPN was neutralized during HBV antigen stimulation [9].
- Human PBMC-derived CD14+ macrophages were used to study polarization in response to osteoarthritis synovial fluid, but the specific contribution of OPN was not isolated [15].
- Bone matrix constituents (collagen fragments, hydroxyapatite) stimulated IL-1 release from human PBMC, indicating that mineral/matrix context can activate PBMC cytokine pathways [30].
- **Gap:** Direct dose–response studies in which purified OPN is added to human PBMCs to measure cytokine output (IL-12, IFN-γ, IL-10, TNF-α, IL-6, etc.) are scarce; no such study with yeast-expressed human OPN was found.

### 3.4 Th1/Th2 context and a contradictory finding

- Early work characterized OPN as a Th1 cytokine that enhances IFN-γ and IL-12 while suppressing IL-10, and OPN deficiency is linked to reduced Th1 responses in autoimmunity and infection [2][8].
- However, Abel et al. reported that OPN is **not required** for the development of Th1 responses and viral immunity in lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus and vaccinia virus models, challenging the universality of the Th1 hypothesis [29].
- **Confidence:** The Th1-promoting effect is well supported in DC/T-cell co-culture and autoimmune models [8][10]; the dispensability of OPN for antiviral Th1 immunity [29] means claims should be qualified by pathogen/model.

## 4. Bone mineralization and remodeling

### 4.1 OPN as a mineralization regulator

- OPN is a potent inhibitor of hydroxyapatite crystal formation/growth in vitro [17][19].
- OPN-deficient (Opn−/−) mice develop a postnatal hypermineralized bone phenotype starting at ~12 weeks [19].
- Supplementation of Opn−/− osteoblast cultures with bovine milk OPN or OPN-derived peptides (phosphorylated OPN220-235 and non-phosphorylated OPN65-80) rescued the hypermineralization phenotype without altering terminal osteogenic marker expression, suggesting OPN regulates mineralization rather than osteoblast differentiation per se [19].
- In Phospho1−/− mice, elevated phosphorylated OPN contributes to skeletal abnormalities; genetic ablation of OPN improves the phenotype, consistent with OPN acting as a mineralization inhibitor [20].

### 4.2 Role in bone wound healing/remodeling

- In a rat bone-drill wound model, macrophage-secreted OPN bound to exposed mineralized surfaces and bone debris (via calcium-binding phosphorylations and net negative charge), acting as an opsonin for macrophage phagocytosis of debris [18].
- OPN deposited at cement lines by macrophages and later by osteoblast lineage cells contributes to cell adhesion, signaling, and matrix mineralization at the bone–bone interface [18].
- The stenciling principle of bone mineralization posits that mineralization is controlled by inhibitors (e.g., pyrophosphate, OPN) and enzymes (e.g., TNAP, PHEX) that degrade them; OPN is explicitly named as one of the protein inhibitors [21].

### 4.3 CKD-mineral bone disorder context

- OPN has been implicated in disturbances of bone mineralization and remodeling and interacts with parathyroid hormone and FGF23 in experimental models; in CKD patients with severe hyperparathyroidism, muscle OPN expression correlated with worse functionality and local inflammation [22].

### 4.4 Confidence and gaps

- OPN’s role as a mineralization inhibitor is **High** confidence [17][19][20][21].
- The distinction between “regulates mineralization” versus “regulates osteoblast differentiation” is important and supported by in vitro data [19]; however, whether yeast-expressed human OPN retains the same phosphorylation status and mineral-inhibitory potency is unverified.
- Bone-forming/anabolic effects attributed to OPN in some tissue-engineering contexts may reflect cell-adhesion/signaling rather than direct pro-mineralization activity; this should not be conflated with its mineral-inhibitory role.

## 5. Integrin/CD44 interaction details

### 5.1 Integrin binding

- OPN binds integrin αvβ3 via the RGD motif to support osteoclast motility, podosome disassembly, and actin reorganization at the leading edge [23].
- RGD-containing peptides mimic and anti-αvβ3 antibody blocks OPN-stimulated osteoclast motility [23].
- Thrombin cleavage of OPN exposes the SVVYGLR sequence, which is a high-affinity ligand for integrin α9β1; the tyrosine residue (Tyr165 in human OPN) within SVVYGLR is critical [26].
- Polymeric OPN can generate a de novo α9β1-binding site and attract neutrophils [28].

### 5.2 CD44 binding

- CD44 binding to OPN is moderate for unmodified soluble OPN and is strongly enhanced by structural constraint (immobilization) or heparin, which induces partial unfolding of OPN [27].
- The CD44 contact site appears to lie C-terminal to the RGD motif and overlaps with the SVVYGLR region; αvβ3 binding is additive with CD44 binding, whereas α9β1 can compete with CD44 [27].
- CD44 variant isoforms (CD44v) have been reported to cooperate with β1-containing integrins to permit OPN binding independently of RGD and to stimulate cell motility/chemotaxis [27].

### 5.3 Downstream signaling consequences

| Pathway / process | Cell type | Receptor involvement | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autophagy stimulation | Vascular smooth muscle cells | Integrin/CD44 → p38 MAPK | [24] |
| Autophagy inhibition | Osteoarthritic chondrocytes | CD44 + αvβ3 integrin → ERK MAPK | [25] |
| Intestinal proliferation/differentiation | Jejunal enterocytes | Integrin αvβ3 + CD44 → ERK, PI3K/Akt, Wnt, FAK | [5] |
| Osteoclast motility | Osteoclasts | αvβ3 + CD44 | [23] |

- **Contradiction:** OPN can either stimulate [24] or inhibit [25] autophagy depending on cell type and MAPK branch, illustrating that receptor context (and possibly OPN modification state) determines downstream outcome.

## 8. Summary of contradictions and data gaps

### 8.1 Contradictions / context-dependent findings

1. **Macrophage polarization:** OPN has been reported to have no pro-inflammatory polarizing effect in human adipose macrophages [12], to shift microglia toward M2 [13], and to drive M2/type-2 responses in fungal infection [14]. A single M1/M2 label is therefore unreliable.
2. **DC function:** OPN activates DCs and promotes Th1 polarization [8] but also suppresses CCR7-mediated DC migration and T-cell priming [11], and DC-derived OPN suppresses Th2 responses via IL-12 [10].
3. **Autophagy:** OPN stimulates autophagy in vascular smooth muscle cells [24] but inhibits autophagy in osteoarthritic chondrocytes [25].
4. **Th1 immunity:** OPN is described as a Th1-promoting cytokine [2][8], yet it is dispensable for antiviral Th1 responses in some models [29].

### 8.2 Data gaps

- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests **yeast-expressed human OPN** in intestinal barrier, moDC, macrophage, PBMC, or bone-mineralization assays.
- Direct human PBMC stimulation with purified OPN and receptor-blocking antibodies is under-represented.
- The impact of yeast-specific glycosylation/phosphorylation patterns on OPN receptor binding (integrin αvβ3, α9β1, CD44) and functional potency is unknown.
- FSANZ-specific regulatory status for OPN was not located.
- FDA GRN 716 for bovine milk OPN was withdrawn; there is no FDA GRAS affirmation for OPN in infant formula in the US.
